Abstract

Injury-aware falling and recovery

Humanoid robots fall when balance recovery fails, and most controllers treat that as the end of the task. We handle it instead: Ukemi-SafeFall learns to reduce the mechanical damage of an unavoidable fall while keeping the robot able to stand up afterward. In simulation it cuts normalized peak contact force by 32%, cumulative peak contact force by 78% and unsafe-contact events by 70% compared with passive falling, and it transfers to a physical humanoid across a range of falling scenarios.

Condition-gated

Roll and recover

Rolling and recovery rewards switch on automatically from torso tilt and head height, so no separate phase estimator is needed.

Impact mitigation

Shape contact forces

Training penalizes heavy loading on the trunk and arms, which lowers peak contact force and the number of unsafe contacts.

Motion prior

Stay natural

A frozen Score-Matching Motion Prior scales the reward, keeping the learned motion close to natural human behavior.

Method

Condition-gated rewards with a motion prior

We train a policy with Proximal Policy Optimization. Its reward multiplies condition-gated task rewards (safe rolling, impact mitigation, standing up) by a reward from a diffusion-based Score-Matching Motion Prior. The same prior also generates diverse fallen starting poses for training.

Results

Comparison with baselines and ablations

All methods are evaluated on the same fallen-state protocol. We compare against Passive Fall, remove one component at a time in three ablations, and include a re-implemented SafeFall baseline as a reference.

Keeps the gated task rewards but removes the motion-prior reward gate.
